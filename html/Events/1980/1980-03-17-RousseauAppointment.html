03/17/1980: Rousseau Appointment

Summary:

Rousseau confronts Regulus about his plan to reveal his identity to the press. Regulus is still under the influence of veritaserum.

Regulus admits that he can't tell James about his plan, but he needs to reveal his identity in order to continue living. Rousseau intentionally stops leading the discussion, prompting Regulus to ask about Memory Recovery.

Regulus asks to start memory recovery from the partially-recovered memory of James and Regulus meeting in the Astronomy Tower in sixth year. Regulus briefly questions whether he should start from the memory of his and James' first kiss, since that's what he promised James he would remember. Rousseau gives noncommital answers until finally Regulus explodes.

Once recovered, Regulus begins to grapple with the fact that James technically saved The Dagger's life. He starts to say that James should hate him for the Mark, but remembers that James offered to kiss along it very recently. Rousseau points out that initiation was a matter of life and death, and that Regulus had little choice in what he had to do to achieve it.

Regulus starts to claim that he's always been alone, but when asked to name an example, he sees that he's never been without support. Rousseau says that 'it's unreasonable to expect anyone to go through life alone."

Regulus circles back to the press conference, admitting that he's afraid James will tell him not to go through with it. Rousseau asks if it will be easier to be known as Rodolphus' slave than it would be to be known as one of his clients. Regulus responds that it will make it easier to look James in the eye, and that it will be better for everyone else.

Rousseau points out that Regulus spends a lot of time labeling things as 'good' and 'bad', and asks him why that is. They trace the impulse back chronologically, finding that Regulus believed he would be disowned if he wasn't good enough. Rousseau leaves Regulus with the question, 'Who told you what it meant to be good?'
